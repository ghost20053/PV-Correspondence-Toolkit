EXEGESIS CHAPTER 5 · TABLE 1

Research Findings and Design Contribution

Four interconnected findings emerged from one developer's documented, iterative practice across three Action Research cycles. They are researcher observations and design interpretations, not experimentally validated guidelines, universal rules or results from independent participant testing.

Table 1 synthesises these findings into six reflective design questions. The links below preserve the table's mapping while showing where the development reasoning can be followed in the Reflective Development Journal.

5.1 Correspondence Depends on the Intended Action

Evaluate the physical contact, grasp, placement and movement an action requires. Visual similarity alone cannot establish a useful physical counterpart. Pinch-grab offsets and later cube and chest testing made this distinction consequential.

Table 1: Intended Action, Physical Counterpart and Correspondence.

Evidence trail: journal Sections 3–4, 7, 11 and 19; Videos 01–02 and 05; physical-prop photographs. Follow Cycle One and Cycle Two.

5.2 Calibration and Onboarding Form One Design Problem

Fake-hand targets could trigger calibration while requiring a reach away from the actual tabletop. The later two-hand tabletop action, passthrough and instructions joined alignment to a setup the wearer could physically perform.

Table 1: Onboarding.

Evidence trail: journal Sections 13, 15–17 and 20–21; Figure 4 and calibration recordings. Read the completed calibration example or browse calibration screenshots and videos.

5.3 Controlled Physical Layout Can Substitute for Missing Object-State Data

The marked cloth defined repeatable starting positions for the hands, cubes and chest. This reduced uncertainty within a bounded setup; it did not let the engine detect independently moved props or continuously track their orientation.

Table 1: Starting Arrangement.

Evidence trail: journal Sections 11.1, 12 and 19; Figures 3–4 and the paired final recordings in Section 24. Follow the physical-layout evidence and read the setup guide.

5.4 Tracking Constraints Shape Tangible Interaction

Occlusion, gesture differences and attachment mismatches influenced the interactions selected. The system tracked hands but could not independently sense untracked prop state. These are situated observations, not measured tracking-accuracy results.

Table 1: System Knowledge.

Evidence trail: journal Sections 7, 9, 11 and 23; Video 05 and headset recordings. Browse interaction recordings.
